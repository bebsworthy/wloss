# Gyroscope — Feature Analysis

**Category**: Quantified-self / all-in-one health tracking, data-visualization dashboard, AI health coaching ("a new operating system for the human body")
**Platforms**: iOS (iPhone + Apple Watch; App Store release Apr 30, 2016; version 10.11 as of Aug 2026), web dashboards at gyrosco.pe, Chrome extension ("every new tab"), Mac desktop app (beta). No Android app.
**Pricing model**: Subscription-only once past the free tier. Free: basics (Food XRAY camera, simple timeline) with 30 days of data storage. Gyroscope One ("about $1 a day"; user reviews cite the climb $12 → $15 → $25 → $39.95/month and a $179/year annual): Health Score, AI coach, Food XRAY, custom workouts/meditations, wearable sync, bodyfat protocol, unlimited storage, blood & genetics. Gyroscope Max (top tier): daily cash back for hitting targets, up to $5/day AI tokens, "extra-large models", ultrafast AI mode, Quarterly Data Review (printable "book of your life"), early access (desktop app, MCP bridge beta). Add-ons: Places Unlimited, discounted blood tests, physical merch (shirt), printed reports.
**AI usage**: Pervasive and cloud-based: Food XRAY photo food recognition ("combines photos, text labels, scale data, nutrition labels and years of data"), AI coach chat trained on your biometrics, auto-generated protocols and custom workouts/meditations, weekly Health Score computation, early-warning detection, V8 "AI Health Camera" (photograph anything to log it), larger proprietary models (named "Fable", "Sol") for Max members.
**Local & privacy posture**: Cloud-first, account required ("Gyroscope requires an account to sync and view your data"). Positioning: "Your data will NEVER be sold… We also show no ads. We only make money through the monthly subscriptions." Private by default with opt-in social (friends/public profile); full account deletion at gyrosco.pe/delete. Data is explicitly not local — the service exists to aggregate and store your life history server-side. This is the exact inverse of WLO's architecture.

## Overview

Gyroscope began in 2014 as "April Zero," founder Anand Sharma's public quantified-self experiment and one of the most-discussed personal dashboards ever built; it became a funded startup in 2015 (True Ventures is an investor and is quoted on the site). Over a decade it evolved from a passive, gorgeous aggregation dashboard ("the magic of it is how it just figures out everything based on data you already provide" — TechCrunch) into a full AI-coaching product. The team is deliberately small (founder, engineers, a strength coach, a nurse coach) and unusually transparent, publishing a public timeline of every release since 2014 and a "Transparency: behind the scenes" page.

The product concept is that your body is hardware running an outdated operating system, and Gyroscope is the patch: it unifies sleep, heart, steps, food, workouts, body fat, blood, glucose, mood, meditation, travel, and even computer/screen time into one automatically-maintained dashboard, then compresses it into a weekly-refreshed Health Score, letter-grade "report cards," early warnings, and coach guidance. Its stated audience is "busy people" — founders, doctors, engineers — and its own reviews say the quiet part out loud: "There are a lot of details and features so it might be best for quantitative personality types." That is precisely WLO's target user.

Market position: the design benchmark for health-data visualization ("Want to start self-tracking but unsure where to store your data, or how to make it look good?" — Fitt Insider; "This is where every piece of data I collect comes together in a unified dashboard" — MacStories), a #1 Product of the Day on Product Hunt, and a long-lived subscription business that survived the death of most quantified-self apps by pivoting from dashboard to coach. For WLO it is the reference for dashboard card design, data storytelling, health scoring, and numbers-geek gamification — and a cautionary tale about pricing churn and feature sprawl.

## Feature inventory

### Photos / silhouette / body scanning

N/A — not offered as a 3D body scan. Body composition is tracked via smart-scale data or "a photo of it": "Bodyfat: weight and bodyfat, from a scale or a photo of the display" — photographing a bathroom scale's readout is an official input path. The broader photo philosophy ("the world's first Health Camera… tracking your life is as simple as taking a photo") covers food, scale displays, blood-pressure cuffs, and thermometers, but there is no silhouette/avatar/body-shape visualization.

### Data sources & integrations

- Wearables & devices: Apple Watch (sleep stages, HRV, workouts, blood oxygen, wrist temperature), Oura, Garmin, Whoop, Withings and any Wi-Fi scale, Omron/Withings blood-pressure cuffs, Polar/Wahoo chest straps, Stelo and Dexcom CGMs, any thermometer (photographed).
- Platforms: Apple Health, "thousands of integrations," RescueTime and Last.fm (named in user reviews), Mac desktop app for computer time/focus, Chrome extension.
- Genetics: 23andMe / Ancestry import for "genetic insights" feeding the coach.
- Clinical: Gyroscope blood tests (discounted with membership) with manual import from any lab; blood panel tracking (ApoB, LDL, HDL, triglycerides, HbA1c, fasting glucose) "tracked over years."
- Passive auto-tracking: Places (automatic travel/place timeline), phone/screen-time metrics, mood/energy/stress check-ins.

### Statistics, visualization & gamification (CRITICAL)

- **The card/dashboard language**: "One dashboard pulls your whole body together and maintains itself." Six core metric cards — Sleep, Heart, Steps, Food, Workouts, Bodyfat — each a single tile summarizing the day/week, drilling down into a specific metric list (Sleep → duration, deep, REM, sleep debt, bedtime, wake time; Heart → resting HR, HRV, VO2 max, cardio fitness, HR zones, blood pressure; Food → calories, protein, fiber, carbs, fat, sugar; Bodyfat → weight, body fat, lean mass, muscle mass, waist, BMI). Dozens of secondary domains (glucose, blood, digital/screen time, mind/mood, places) follow the same card pattern. Historically these cards are the app's signature: vivid, chart-led, self-contained tiles users screenshot and share ("2017 Reports in the wild: some images shared by users, and winners of the free T-shirt contest").
- **Health Score**: "the most important number in your life, refreshed weekly from your biometrics" — one score unifying all metrics and devices, with marketing that ties it to longevity ("shows how long you will live… estimated lifespan: your habits, in years"). Introduced June 2019 ("Meet your Health Score").
- **Report card**: "grades for every system" — letter grades per body system, the school-report metaphor applied to biology; plus **early warnings** "before you feel it" (a member: "several times I saw a flu or cold coming in Gyroscope while I did not have the complaints yet") and **continuous monitoring** of blood levels and glucose.
- **Chart & data storytelling**: long-horizon trend views ("look back for decades"), correlations ("everything is connected" philosophy page), week-in-review and coach-written narrative insights ("a coach that reads your real data"), and — uniquely — physical data artifacts: printed **Annual Report books** shipped to users since 2017 ("The story of designing & printing personalized books for all our users"), succeeded by the Max-tier **Quarterly Data Review: "a curated book of your life with a new chapter every quarter. In the app and as a printable PDF."** The homepage itself is a masterclass in numbered-chapter storytelling (01 Tracking / 02 Insights / 03 Coaching) with organ-as-hardware illustrations (brain, heart, liver) and big aggregate stats (47.2% sleep improved, 69.2% lost bodyfat, 86.8% eating better).
- **Gamification**: a decade-deep stack — Gyroscope Multiplayer (2016) "a new way to compete with your friends"; Goals (2016) "don't just view your stats — change them!"; "The Game of Life" (2019) "a fun way to level up your character in real life"; challenges and streaks (reviews: "gamified challenges that move the needle", V9's "personalized nudges and reminders"); food letter-grades per meal (Food XRAY grades every meal); benchmarks — physical tests (pull-ups, push-ups, plank, grip, VO2 max, BOLT score, sit-stand, balance) and cognitive reaction mini-games (Flash Tap, Color Clash, Number Trail, N-Back Grid…) used to "establish a baseline" and "see how your results change"; and at Max tier literal money: "daily cash back — get real dollars back for every day you check in and hit your targets."

### AI features (on-device vs cloud)

- All AI is cloud-side; there is no on-device ML story. Food XRAY: photograph any meal → macros, micros, and a grade, claimed "the most accurate" photo tracker by fusing photos with text labels and scale data. V8 (Mar 2025) generalized this into an "AI Health Camera" — photograph food, a scale, a thermometer, a BP cuff, or a lab result to log it.
- AI coach: 24/7 chat "written from your sleep, food, workouts, blood and where you are today"; generates protocols (the "bodyfat protocol: twelve requirements tracked for you"), custom workout programs ("a trainer that logs every set"), and fully customized meditations. Max tier ups the model size ("Fable", "Sol"), adds "Ultrafast Mode" priority processing, a daily token budget, and an MCP bridge beta (exposing member data to external AI agents).
- Gyroscope Labs: a formal A/B framework for self-experiments — "test any change to your lifestyle and see what it does to your actual body," from a library of study-backed habits, with per-habit ratings ("felt an effect", "easy to do").

### Input-minimization techniques

- Photo-first everything: meals, scale displays, thermometer readings, blood-pressure cuffs, lab results — "photo over manual entry" as a product-wide doctrine.
- Automatic sync from thousands of devices so "you can spend your time being healthy instead of fiddling with settings"; the app "maintains itself."
- Passive capture: places/travel auto-detected; computer time from the desktop app; mood/meditation via quick check-ins.
- Even with no devices at all, "the app can measure essentials like mood, calories, protein, travels, sleep, food" — a floor of camera+manual-light input.

### Design & UX / micro-interactions

- **Design language**: clean, typographic, chart-forward cards with strong color identity; the site brand is modernist — oversized display type, gradient heroes, numbered chapters, monospaced data accents, organ illustrations as "hardware." The app has cycled through several visual generations (V3 2019, V5 2022, V8 2025, V9 2026) but consistently keeps the shareable single-card metaphor: every metric can stand alone as a beautiful image. This is the core of its organic marketing — the UI is the ad.
- **Motion**: an engineering-culture commitment to animation quality — the team published "10 principles for smooth web animations: the complete guide to 60fps smooth animations" (2016); transitions and count-up numerals are part of the premium feel.
- **Internationalization**: 25+ languages shipped on the marketing site, unusual for a small team.
- **Dark, cinematic presentation** has been the default aesthetic across versions, tuned for glanceability of a single hero number per card.
- **Friction**: reviewers say the modern app is "way too busy with information and not optimally placed," features are "buried in places that aren't intuitive," and plan/feature churn ("they have never really landed on a plan, feature set, or interface") has eroded the once-lucid design. One legacy complaint: the app crashed at launch under iOS VoiceOver (accessibility gap).
- **Premium feel mechanics**: physical artifacts (printed books, shirt), human coaches, cash-back, and price itself — the ~$30/month cost is part of the luxury positioning ("It is expensive, but in my opinion, it's worth it").

## Strengths & differentiators

1. **The card as a unit of storytelling**: self-contained, chart-led, shareable metric cards + a single unifying Health Score — a decade of refinement that still defines what "beautiful quantified-self UI" means.
2. **Grades, scores, and warnings as narrative**: letter-grade report cards, weekly score refresh, early-warning language ("before you feel it") — numbers turned into a story you act on.
3. **Physical/durable data artifacts**: printed annual report books (since 2017) and quarterly "book of your life" chapters — the strongest long-term-retention and emotional-payoff mechanic in the genre; users cite 7-10+ years of continuous use.
4. **Photo-camera-as-sensor doctrine** (V8 Health Camera): one capture gesture (a photo) covers food, weight, vitals, and labs — maximal input minimization.
5. **Benchmarks & self-experimentation**: repeated physical/cognitive tests plus Gyroscope Labs habit trials give a numbers-geek something to *do* with the data, not just admire it.
6. **Trust framing**: no ads, no data sale, subscription-funded, transparent public roadmap/timeline, one-tap full deletion — a coherent premium-privacy narrative (albeit cloud-based).
7. **Longevity framing**: estimated-lifespan and blood-marker integration lift the product from fitness tracking to life extension, deepening willingness to pay.

## Weaknesses & user complaints (App Store user reviews via iTunes RSS, 2025-2026; official site)

- **Price escalation and legacy-tier erosion** is the loudest theme: "cost is now out of control — they want to force you into a $40/month plan; legacy pricing ($15) has eliminated features like the health score… to force higher pricing"; "the $99/year tier's features reduced after each update"; "$179/year members find the coaches blocked behind a new tier"; "$12 → $15 → $25, I pulled the plug."
- **Plan and feature churn**: "they have never really landed on a plan, feature set, or interface… constantly encouraged to switch plans with drastically different features"; "further development looks very inconsistent, with constant shuffle of features."
- **Clutter & discoverability**: "too busy with information and not optimally placed"; "many features are buried in places that aren't intuitive, and I often find myself hunting for something I know exists."
- **Accessibility**: VoiceOver users could not launch the app at all (crash loop).
- **Architecture**: requires an account and cloud sync (no offline/local-first mode); iOS-only (Android users excluded entirely); individual logging bugs reported (checkmarks not registering for protein/meditation).
- **Positioning tension**: the "beautiful dashboard" that long-time users loved has been progressively de-emphasized in favor of coaching — several reviews mourn the original product.

## What WLO should learn

1. **One hero number per card, a drill-down per card, and a weekly composite score.** Adopt Gyroscope's three-layer pyramid: daily metric cards (sleep-equivalents for WLO: calories, macros, exercise minutes, weight trend, silhouette delta, Bristol log) → a weekly "report card" with letter grades per domain → one composite WLO Score refreshed on a fixed cadence. Fixed cadence matters ("refreshed weekly") — it turns heavy computation into an anticipated event instead of a background churn.
2. **Design every card to be screenshotted.** Gyroscope's growth loop is users sharing their report cards and annual-report images ("Reports in the wild"). Give each WLO card a stable share layout — fixed aspect ratio, big numeral, gradient chart, wordmark — and a native share sheet. In a local-first app this is free viral marketing that leaks no data (the card, not the database, is what leaves the phone).
3. **Ship a data artifact, not just charts.** The printed annual report book and quarterly "chapter" PDF are Gyroscope's most beloved mechanic and its strongest retention glue. A local-first WLO can render the same thing on-device: a monthly/annual "journey book" PDF combining weight trend, silhouette evolution (photo strip/avatars), diet adherence, and exercise milestones — exportable, printable, and owned by the user.
4. **Grade and narrate, don't just plot.** Letter grades (A–F per meal/day/domain), "early warning" phrasing, and one-sentence natural-language insights ("sleep debt is driving your calorie overage") make numbers actionable for non-statisticians without dumbing them down for geeks. WLO can generate these on-device with templates/rules, or via the user's optional remote AI key for richer prose — provider-agnostic, exactly Gyroscope's coach pattern minus the cloud lock-in.
5. **Photo-of-display is a universal input trick.** Gyroscope logs weight and body fat by photographing a scale screen, and vitals by photographing thermometers and BP cuffs. This is cheap to implement (crop + OCR on-device), fits WLO's "photo+recognition over manual input" principle perfectly, and eliminates the most-hated entry type (typing numbers off a device display).
6. **Gamify with tests and money-like stakes, not badges.** Gyroscope's best engagement mechanics are measurable self-tests (plank, push-ups, reaction-time mini-games) with baselines and deltas, streaky daily check-ins, and (at Max) cash-back for hitting targets. WLO equivalents: weekly strength/fitness benchmark tests, weigh-in and meal-log streaks with meaningful milestone rewards, and "level up your character" framing for the weight-loss avatar — all computable on-device.
7. **Anti-patterns to avoid (Gyroscope's late-stage complaints):** never remove or re-gate features a paying/free user already has; never rename/re-shuffle tiers more than rarely; resist feature sprawl that buries core flows — WLO's minimalist information architecture is a competitive weapon against exactly this complaint; keep navigation to one dashboard + one entry gesture; fund the app with a simple, stable model and say so in the UI ("you are the customer, not the product" is a one-line trust asset worth copying verbatim in spirit).
8. **Local-first is the differentiator Gyroscope can't follow.** Its account/cloud requirement (and fear of losing history on reinstall — the thing its own FAQ sells against) is structural. WLO should guarantee: full functionality offline, history that lives and dies with the user's device (with explicit encrypted backup), no account required — and make that guarantee visible on the dashboard itself, the way Gyroscope advertises "data never sold."
9. **Motion and typography are identity.** Gyroscope's team treated 60fps animation as an engineering principle, and it shows in perceived quality. Budget real time for WLO's micro-interactions: animated count-ups on hero numerals, spring transitions between card and drill-down, haptics on grade reveals, skeleton-overlay capture flow (shared with the MeThreeSixty lessons). Dark-first, chart-forward, oversized numerals is the aesthetic target audience already associates with "serious tracking."
10. **Longevity/health-span framing deepens engagement** ("estimated lifespan: your habits, in years"). WLO can offer trend-to-forecast storytelling (its forecasting feature) with careful, range-based language — aspirational but honest — rather than pseudo-precise claims.
